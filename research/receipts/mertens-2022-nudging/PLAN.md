## Method

run.R, run from the checkout's root as `Rscript research/receipts/mertens-2022-nudging/run.R`, does the following and nothing else.

1. Checks the two inputs it uses against their fingerprints, installs the four vendored packages into /tmp and confirms that metafor 3.0-2 loaded.
2. Sets R's random seed from ECDYSIS_SEED (its first seven hexadecimal digits). Nothing in the parts of the script the test uses is random; the seed is set so that, if anything is, it comes from the archive's seed only. If ECDYSIS_SEED is missing or not hexadecimal, run.R stops with no outputs before anything else is done; the archive's seed is 64 hexadecimal characters, and the runner always sets it. (Amended 9 October 2026: run.R previously went on unseeded if the variable was empty.)
3. Empties a writable folder under /tmp, copies the corrected data into it, stops with no outputs if the folder then holds anything but that one file, and runs the authors' script there, unchanged, from its first line to its last. The script reads its data by name from the folder it runs in, which is why it runs in a copy of that folder. Two things are changed around it, neither inside it: the script's eight View() calls, which open a spreadsheet window that a sealed machine does not have and would otherwise stop the run, do nothing; and its plots go to no file.
4. If the script stops with an error at any point, run.R stops and writes no outputs, whether or not the two named models exist by then: the test says "Inconclusive if the run stops or times out". (Amended 9 October 2026: the plan as approved carried on to the comparison when the script stopped after both models existed, which narrowed the registered rule while calling it unchanged. The script's last part, after domain_robust, includes plots and further models, and the real run must now get through all of it; the end-to-end test shows that it does on synthetic data.)
5. Checks the publication years the script read (the df$year column): every year must be present, a finite whole number, and within 1900 to 2100, a plain sanity range; otherwise run.R stops with no outputs. These years give period_from and period_to. If the years span anything other than 1982 to 2021, the run is not stopped and the outcome is still judged by the test, but the difference is put to the operator before anything is filed. (Added 9 October 2026: the period was computed without checks, so a missing year could have made it null while the outcome was still confirmed.)
6. Reads the seven figures from the two named models, finding each row by its coefficient's name (compare.R): primary_robust's intercept ("intrcpt"), and domain_robust's six rows "factor(domain)health", "factor(domain)food", "factor(domain)environment", "factor(domain)finance", "factor(domain)pro-social" and "factor(domain)other" (the data's own labels). The domain model has no intercept and its year term is centred (scale(year)), so each domain row is that domain's estimate at the mean publication year, which is what Table 2 reports; the year coefficient is not compared. A missing name stops the run with no outputs. A figure or count that is absent, not a number, or (for a count) more than one value is treated as not finite, which the test makes inconclusive. (Amended 9 October 2026: an absent or two-valued count would have stopped the run with an R error instead.)
7. Rounds each estimate and each 95% CI limit half away from zero to two decimals, applying the rule to the value exactly as computed, with no tolerance, and compares the 21 numbers with Table 2, as compare.R does. R's own round() rounds half to even, so it is not used. Nor is the usual arithmetic, floor(abs(x) * 100 + 0.5), because the multiplication itself rounds: the computed value nearest 0.435 lies just below 0.435 and so rounds to 0.43, but 0.435 * 100 gives exactly 43.5 in floating point, and the arithmetic gives 0.44. compare.R instead writes the stored value out in full (glibc's printf gives a double's exact decimal expansion), keeps the digits to the second decimal, and adds 0.01 if the third decimal digit is 5 or more. This departs, with reason, from PLAYBOOK.md's data-handling rule 5, which asks for an explicit tolerance at thresholds: the registered test says half away from zero and nothing more, and an estimate landing within a hair of a tie is very unlikely. (Amended 9 October 2026: the code as approved added 1e-9 after scaling, so a value within about 1e-11 below a tie rounded up; neither this plan nor the test mentions such an allowance.)

**Outputs** (18, plus period_from and period_to): k_primary and k_domain; for each of the seven figures, est_<figure> and hw_<figure> (the half-width of its 95% interval: robust() gives the estimate plus or minus t times its standard error, so the interval is symmetric and is recovered exactly from the two); mismatches (how many of the 21 compared numbers differ after rounding); and mismatch_mask, an integer whose bits mark which of the 21 differ: bit 1 (worth 1) to bit 7 for the seven estimates, bits 8 to 14 for the lower limits and bits 15 to 21 for the upper limits, each in Table 2's order (overall, health, food, environment, finance, pro-social, other), so that a failure can be traced to its numbers from the record. (Amended 9 October 2026: script_completed, which recorded whether the script ran to its last line, always equals 1 now that any error stops the run, so it was dropped, and mismatch_mask takes its place.) The full report of 7 estimates, 14 limits and 2 counts would be 23 numbers, above the platform's limit of 20, which is why half-widths are reported in place of the limits. period_from and period_to are the first and last publication years in the data, as 1 January and 31 December of those years (the claim's period is of publication years). A cross-check compares estimates and half-widths within 1e-9, and the counts, mismatches and mismatch_mask exactly.

**Run time:** declared as 60 minutes (runtimeMinutes 60; the runner stops a run at three times that). The end-to-end test on synthetic data of the real file's size took 35 minutes in the sealed setup, with the runner's limits of 2 CPUs and 4 GB.

## Tests

test_run.R, run by test.sh in the pinned image sealed as the reference runner seals a bundle. Part 1 tests the comparison on made-up model results: the published figures are confirmed; figures within rounding of them are confirmed; each of the 21 compared numbers moved alone by 0.006 costs exactly one mismatch; ties round away from zero (0.335 to 0.34) and 0.3349999 does not; a wrong k in either model is a refutation; a figure of NaN, Inf or NA, or a missing count, is inconclusive; a coefficient not found by name stops the run; the outputs number at most 20. (Tests added 9 October 2026: every tie next to one of the 21 published numbers, as the computer stores it, rounds as Python's exact decimal arithmetic says it should, and so do the values one step above and below each tie, and their negatives; 0.435 and 0.345, stored below their ties, round down, where the old arithmetic rounded them up; 0.334999999995 rounds to 0.33; each figure off alone sets its own bit of mismatch_mask; a symmetric interval reproduces environment's printed 0.43 [0.33, 0.54], whose limits look asymmetric only because each was rounded separately; an absent count, a count of two values, a count that is not a number and an absent interval limit are each inconclusive; a missing, zero, fractional, infinite or out-of-range year, or none at all, stops the run; the outputs are exactly the 18 named and the period. Run end to end through run.R: a stand-in script that makes both models and then stops gives no outputs, and so does one that stops at its first line, while the same stand-in without the error gives outputs, so the test tells the old rule from the new; a missing seed and a seed that is not hexadecimal each give no outputs.) Part 2 also runs run.R end to end, with the authors' own script, on synthetic data (synth.R) with the real file's 24 columns, 447 rows from 212 publications (the script's forest plot assumes 447 rows), known true effects by domain, and the clutter real files carry: a byte-order mark, Windows line ends, quoted commas, NA tokens, some variances in E notation, and a hidden file beside the data. It checks that the script runs to its end, that both models use every row, that each domain's true value lies within its estimated interval, that the synthetic figures do not meet Table 2, so the comparison can fail, and (added 9 October 2026) that every estimate and half-width agrees within 1e-8 with the independent implementation's values on the same synthetic data, confirmed to be the same by the file's MD5, so that a wrong but finite coefficient cannot pass.

**What the tests found while the code was written** (8 October 2026, synthetic data only): the wrapper first carried coefficient names into the figures it read, which would have stopped the real run (fixed, and tested); the authors' forest plot (line 259) assumes exactly 447 rows, so the synthetic data have 447; and the script's selmodel() calls need numDeriv, which the image lacked (now vendored). Each would have made the real run stop, and so made the receipt inconclusive.

**Independent re-implementation** (PLAYBOOK.md, statistical practice 7): before approval, a model of another family, working from this plan alone and without sight of run.R or compare.R, writes its own implementation of the two models (three-level random-effects meta-analysis by restricted maximum likelihood, with cluster-robust standard errors by study as metafor 3.0-2's robust() computes them, with and without its small-sample adjustment), and its estimates and limits are compared with run.R's on the synthetic data. The real data are never used for this. Done on 8 October 2026 (independent/comparison.md): OpenAI GPT-6.1 Sol's implementation agreed with run.R on both counts and on every estimate and half-width to within 2e-10. This agreement shows that the code computes what this plan describes; it does not show that the real data give Table 2, which only the run itself can show. (Sentence added 9 October 2026.)

## Checks before the outcome is judged

1. ECDYSIS_SEED is present and hexadecimal. (Added 9 October 2026.)
2. Every input matches its fingerprint and size (the runner, and run.R for the two files it reads).
3. metafor 3.0-2 installs and loads, and the work folder holds the data file alone.
4. The authors' script runs to its last line without an error, both named models then exist, and every compared row is found by name. (Amended 9 October 2026.)
5. Every publication year is present, a whole number and within 1900 to 2100. (Added 9 October 2026.)
6. Every count and compared figure is a finite number.

If any of checks 1 to 5 fails, run.R writes no outputs and the receipt is filed as inconclusive, or as an attempt, saying exactly what stopped it. Check 6 failing is "inconclusive" by the claim's test.

## Outcome rule

The claim's test, unchanged, applied in this order. **Inconclusive** if the run stops or times out, or gives no finite value for a compared figure (the registered words; amended 9 October 2026, since the plan as approved read "if the run stops or times out before both named models are fitted", which was narrower). Otherwise **failed** if k_primary or k_domain is not 447, or if mismatches is above 0. Otherwise **confirmed**. Whatever the outcome, the result is filed.

If the outcome is "failed", the result is still filed by this rule, and the outputs are reported to the operator in full, with the size of each difference, before anyone says what a difference means. A failure can arise from the authors' reporting, from the posted files differing from those they analysed, or from the software versions; the result would say which of these the evidence allows and would claim no more.

## What was already known

The targets are the published figures, so the check is not blind; it can only show whether the posted materials give them. Before this plan, Calopteryx read the authors' script (code only: to name the compared figures, to find what it needs to run, and to find its fixed counts) and, from the data, only the column names, the category labels, the shapes of values in seven columns (for example whether a variance is written in E notation), the number of rows, and the publication and study counts. No model was fitted to the real data, and no estimate was computed or seen. The paper's figures are disputed in three letters in PNAS (2022), which concern how the evidence should be weighed and are untouched by this check.

## Forecast

Probability that the outcome is "confirmed": 0.80. For: the script and data were posted together as the corrected materials, the script's own forest-plot label prints "d = .43 [0.38, 0.48]", matching the corrected table, and metafor's estimates are deterministic given the same version. Against: the corrected abstract and Table 2 disagree on the number of effect sizes (440 against 447), a sign that the correction was not prepared with complete care; a figure lying close to a rounding boundary could round differently under small numerical differences; and the versions the authors used are not stated. This forecast is made before any contact with the results and will not be revised after it.

## How the result is described (operator, 9 October 2026)

In approving, the operator directed that every communication about this receipt, including its methods text, the result and any summary, says that it tests whether the authors' posted data and script reproduce their published figures, and does not test whether choice architecture interventions work. Whatever the outcome, it is never described as evidence for or against the effectiveness of nudging; that question would be a separate piece of work with its own scoping note.
