# Candidate shortlist, 24 October 2026

Drawn up at Lucy's request after she decided not to proceed with Scheibehenne et al. (2010). The criteria she agreed: (1) raw data and analysis code openly published, with a fingerprint that can be recorded; (2) the claim's figures recomputable from them; (3) a clearly stated data period; (4) a later independent test possible. Checked on 24 October 2026 from the papers' data statements, the OSF API, Crossref and OpenAlex. None of these papers or topics is on the Ecdysis record (all 9,104 log entries searched). Nothing has been downloaded or analysed, and no data file has been opened.

## Meets the criteria

1. **Crompton et al. (2025), Nature Human Behaviour, doi:10.1038/s41562-025-02163-z.** Neurodivergence. "Information transfer within and between autistic and non-autistic people." A Registered Report (protocol accepted in principle on 23 August 2022) replicating Crompton et al. (2020): diffusion chains of autistic, non-autistic and mixed-neurotype participants (N = 311). Abstract: "We found no difference in information transfer between single-neurotype and mixed-neurotype chains." Data (DISI_Core_Dataset.csv, with a data dictionary) and the R analysis scripts are on OSF (osf.io/us9c7), as the paper's data and code statements say. Paper CC BY 4.0; abstract deposited with Crossref. Cited 38 times (OpenAlex). Considerations: the participants are autistic and non-autistic adults, so the charter's principles on people described in deficit terms apply, and the data are anonymised participant records to be handled as the charter requires; the claim is a finding of no difference, so the test must say what result would count against it; Lucy to confirm any connection with the authors (Edinburgh-based autism researchers, among others). It is itself a newer test of the 2020 claim, which bears on Lucy's concern about whether older findings still hold.

2. **Roozenbeek et al. (2022), Science Advances, doi:10.1126/sciadv.abo6254.** Misinformation and trust. "Psychological inoculation improves resilience against misinformation on social media." Six randomised studies (n = 6,464) and a YouTube field study (n = 22,632), all preregistered. Data for all studies, a codebook, preregistrations and some scripts (covariate analyses and plots in R; a supplementary Stata do-file) are on OSF (osf.io/3769y). Whether the main analyses are scripted is not yet confirmed. Open access; abstract deposited with Crossref. Cited 471 times. The abstract's findings are stated in words, so the figures for a test come from the results section.

3. **Spitale, Biller-Andorno and Germani (2023), Science Advances, doi:10.1126/sciadv.adh1850.** AI ethics and misinformation. "AI model GPT-3 (dis)informs us better than humans." Preregistered, 697 participants. Anonymised survey data and the scoring notebooks are on OSF (osf.io/9ntgf). Open access; abstract deposited with Crossref. Cited 241 times. The abstract's only figure is the sample size, and its central sentence ("GPT-3 is a double-edge sword") is not itself testable, so a test would rest on a narrower sentence.

## Promising, needs more checking

4. **Campos et al. (2017), Science, doi:10.1126/science.aan5329.** Entrepreneurship. "Teaching personal initiative beats traditional training in boosting small business in West Africa." Randomised trial with 1,500 microenterprise owners in Togo; abstract: "personal initiative training increased firm profits by 30%". The survey data are in the World Bank Microdata Library (catalogue 2860), free after registration; its terms forbid redistribution and allow only aggregate reporting, so receipts would use "registered" access, which Ecdysis weights below open data. The 2017 paper's own code has not been located; a verified reproducibility package exists for the seven-year follow-up on the same firms (World Bank Reproducible Research Repository, catalogue 184). Cited 398 times.

## Considered and set aside

- **Awad et al. (2018), The Moral Machine experiment, Nature.** Data are on OSF (osf.io/3hvt2, about 3 GB), but neither Crossref nor OpenAlex holds its abstract, so Ecdysis could not check a quotation, and the shared code covers figures only.
- **Crompton et al. (2020), Autism.** The OSF project holds the story and scoring scheme only, not the data.
